# Pre-registration M6: where C's saving comes from

- **Registration:** this file is stage 1 of M6's two stages and gets the tag `prereg-m6`. Its reference time is the `created_at` of the GitHub Release `prereg-m6`. That time is recorded with the tag's commit SHA in the Registration table of `docs/notes/env.md`.
- **Normative text:** this English file. `M6.ko.md` is a translation; if they disagree, this file governs.
- **Changes:** this file is not edited after `prereg-m6`. Later changes, and the values that §8 fixes later, go to [AMENDMENTS.md](AMENDMENTS.md), under the next free number (A17, if none is added first; §9).
- **Base:** [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) v1 applies, with the amendments in force (A1 to A16), except where this file says otherwise. Terms are PROTOCOL's, [M2.md](M2.md)'s, [M3.md](M3.md)'s and [M5.md](M5.md)'s.
- **Three hypotheses, on process CPU,** each about one part of the difference between B and C. Everything else M6 measures is reported without a verdict. The results of M1 to M5 are not judged again, whatever M6 finds.

## Disclosure: what was seen before this registration

- **M1, M1.1, M2, M4, M3 and M5:** all of their results. Those that bear on M6's choices. Unless a bullet says otherwise, they are at H1's point (8 channels, main condition) and are process CPU per 120 s window:
  - **B and C:** M1 43.283 s and 18.560 s; M3 44.433 s and 19.490 s; M5 43.733 s and 19.250 s. C's saving was 57.1%, 56.1% and 56.0%.
  - **B's spread:** its sd over 3 runs was 0.34 s (M1), 0.27 s (M1.1), 0.50 s and 0.79 s (M3's two sweeps) and 0.35 s (M5). Between sessions its mean moved by up to 2.7% ([m1_intervals.md](../docs/notes/m1_intervals.md), M3's and M5's reports).
  - **Bd (M1.1):** B 44.04 s, C 19.49 s, Bd 42.79 s, with sd 0.27, 0.26 and 0.67 s over 3 runs. Bd saved 2.8% of B, about 5% of C's saving.
    - **What Bd is:** not only "B without its last copy". Its slots are cached `system-dma32` buffers bound by fd, written by OpenCV inside a `DMA_BUF_IOCTL_SYNC` START/END(WRITE) bracket (A12).
    - **Where its 1.25 s went** ([cpu_breakdown.md](../docs/notes/cpu_breakdown.md)): 0.71 s less in the NPU workers' threads, where B's copy runs; 0.57 s less in the decoder's threads, which do the same work in both arms; 0.03 s more in preprocessing. That note does not explain the decoder's part.
  - **By thread group (the same note):** B's preprocessing threads used 24.55 s (M1) and 24.61 s (M1.1), about 56% of B. Of the 24.72 s C saved in M1, 24.26 s were in those threads. The decoder's threads took 58% to 64% of C's and Cp's process CPU.
  - **Cp (M2),** staggered: 6.07% below C at this point. The NPU workers' threads carried it: 1.01 s of the 1.14 s.
  - **The package (M5):** as installed (P) 26.907 s, restricted to cpu4–7 (P4) 18.113 s, with sd 0.38 s and 0.28 s. The paired difference P4 − P was −8.79 s (descriptive 95% interval ±1.63 s). 42% to 51% of P's CPU seconds were on cpu0–3.
  - **The A55 design (M1.1,** one repetition): with every pipeline thread on cpu0–3, C used 51.7 s and B 150.6 s.
  - **Stage times (M1):** B's preparation takes about 5.1 ms per frame and C's 1.7 ms; B's bind 0.1 ms.
  - **`rknn_run`:** with the clocks fixed, the process CPU of one `rknn_run` in `output_probe` was 0.844 ms, and 0.662 ms without the output cache flush ([m2_exploration.md](../docs/notes/m2_exploration.md)): one context in a probe, not the pipeline. M4 noted that C10's extra CPU fits work inside a longer `rknn_run`.
  - **Cache maintenance (A6's perf runs,** at 1 and 4 channels, default governors): the kernel's cache routines took 8.6% and 9.5% of B's samples and 28.4% and 26.2% of C's.
  - **RGA3 (A4):** it averages a 3 × 3 box moved by one pixel and ignores the interpolation setting; B's luma box was moved to match. A4's probe also tried converting before resizing.
- **libyuv,** read in its source on 2026-11-23 (commit `0ec9f68d82634f6f3414aaaf780e03f9edf002b3`), not run:
  - At an exact 1/3 scale, `ScalePlane` with `kFilterBox` averages a 3 × 3 box in fixed point: the sum times 7281 (65536 / 9, truncated), shifted right by 16, which rounds down. So By's planes are expected to sit about half a level below B's on average, and one level below on flat areas.
  - At that scale its bilinear filter, and every filter of its interleaved-UV and ARGB scalers, is reduced to taking single pixels. So only planar scaling gives B's box; the rounding differs.
- **Not seen:** no run of any of M6's new arms (§1) exists, and no code for them has been written. libyuv has not been built or run on the board, and no tensor of it has been compared with B's.
- **What was chosen after all of this:** the arms (§1), the three hypotheses and their thresholds (§2), and that everything runs at one point.
- **The issues** M6 answers in part: [#62](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/62), [#63](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/63), [#60](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/60), and the question M5's report left open: why the package as installed used about 40% more process CPU than C.

## 1. What is compared

Eight arms at one point, in the same sessions:

| Arm | What runs | New code |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | `bench_main --variant B` | no |
| **Bd** | `--variant Bd` (A12): B's conversion, written straight into slots the NPU reads | no |
| **Rc** | RGA3 converts as in C, into a buffer the CPU can read; `bind` copies it into the bound input with `memcpy`, as B does | a backend |
| **C** | `--variant C` | no |
| **By** | B with its resize and colour conversion done by libyuv instead of OpenCV | a backend |
| **Cp** | `--variant C --post neon` (M2 §1) | no |
| **Cpf** | Cp with the pipeline not pinned: its threads are allowed cpu0–7 | an option |
| **Ct** | C with the thread CPU time of every `rknn_run` recorded | an option |

- **The point:** H1's. 8 channels, main condition: 5 inferences per second per channel, 1 frame in 6 of a 30 fps stream, without the staggered sampler. M1's videos and channel assignment over RTSP, the YOLOv8n model (`04a76611…`), 20 s warm-up and a 120 s window, fixed clocks (A7) and cooling (A9). M6 does not run the auxiliary condition (§7).
- **Three questions, each with its arms:**
  1. **Conversion or copy (#62).** B, Bd, Rc and C:

     | | The CPU copies into the NPU's input | No CPU copy |
     |---|---|---|
     | Conversion on the CPU | B | Bd |
     | Conversion on RGA3 | Rc | C |

  2. **A faster CPU path (#63).** By against B, and C against By.
  3. **What separated the package from C (M5).** C → Cp changes the post-processing to the package's; Cp → Cpf removes the pinning, as the package has none. What is left between Cpf and M5's P is not measured here (§7).
- **And one description (#60):** Ct gives the CPU time inside `rknn_run`, in the pipeline. No hypothesis rests on it.

### The four cells are not a clean factorial

The cells differ in more than "conversion" and "copy". This is how each is built, and §6 names its differences accordingly:

| Cell | Slot memory | CPU cache syncs per frame on the slot | Into the NPU's input |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | the process's own memory | none | `memcpy` into a buffer of `rknn_create_mem` |
| Bd | cached `system-dma32` | START and END(WRITE) | none: the slot is bound by fd |
| Rc | cached `system-dma32` | START and END(READ) | `memcpy` into a buffer of `rknn_create_mem` |
| C | uncached `system-uncached-dma32` | none | none: the slot is bound by fd |

B and Bd also map the decoder's buffer and bracket their read of it with one sync pair (PROTOCOL §5.2, A12); Rc and C do not read it with the CPU.

### The new arms

- **Rc:**
  - **Conversion:** C's own: the same RGA3 call, cores, kernel and letterbox (A1, A4), from the decoder's DRM PRIME buffer.
  - **Destination:** one buffer per input slot from the heap `system-dma32`, which is CPU-cached: the heap of Bd's slots (A12). It is mapped once. If RGA3 cannot write into it, which a functional check before any timed run shows, the heap is `system`, and the amendment of §8 says so.
  - **Copy:** in `bind`, the CPU brackets its read with one `DMA_BUF_IOCTL_SYNC` START(READ) and one END(READ), and copies the slot with `memcpy` into the input buffer bound with `rknn_set_io_mem`: B's binding (A3).
  - **Its tensor** must equal C's byte for byte (§8 step 3).
- **By:**
  - **What changes against B:** only how the 640 × 360 RGB band is made from the decoder's NV12 buffer. B's mapping of the decoder's buffer, its sync bracket, its slot memory, its border and its `bind` with `memcpy` stay.
  - **One configuration,** which keeps B's steps and replaces OpenCV's calls by libyuv's:
    1. **Luma:** `ScalePlane` with `kFilterBox`, on the frame shifted by one pixel as B's `resize_luma` shifts it (A4): from the region starting at column 1 and row 1, 1917 × 1077, to 639 × 359. The last row and the last column are computed as B's `resize_luma` computes them, by a copy of its code in By's file: the mean of input columns 3c+1 to 3c+3 and rows 3r+1 to 3r+3, with the frame's last row and column repeated, rounded as (sum + 4) / 9. `cpu_common.cpp` does not change.
    2. **Chroma:** `SplitUVPlane` of the 960 × 540 interleaved plane, then `ScalePlane` with `kFilterBox` on U and on V, to 320 × 180 each, without a shift, as B scales chroma.
    3. **Colour:** `I420ToRAW` on the three small planes, into the band of the slot: BT.601 limited range, bytes in the order R, G, B.
  - **libyuv:** commit `0ec9f68d82634f6f3414aaaf780e03f9edf002b3` of `https://chromium.googlesource.com/libyuv/libyuv`, built from source and not installed as a system package. It is added with `add_subdirectory(… EXCLUDE_FROM_ALL)`, and By's target links its static library `yuv`, which has its NEON code for AArch64. libyuv's own CMake options stay at their defaults; the root's build type and compile options apply to it as to every target. "Does not build" means that `yuv` or By's target fails to compile or link. If that commit does not build on the board, stage 2 takes the nearest earlier commit that does, before any tensor is compared, and the amendment says so.
  - **Equivalence:** By's tensors and detections are compared with B's under A5's criteria (§8 step 3). If By does not pass, it is not run, and §2 says what follows. No other configuration is tried.
  - **Threads:** as B, `--prep-threads` at its default; libyuv starts no thread.
- **Cpf:**
  - **A new option** of `bench_main`, `--cores all`: before any library starts a thread, the process is allowed cpu0–7, in place of cpu4–7. The metrics thread stays on cpu0, as in every arm. "Pipeline threads" are all threads but the metrics thread.
  - **Where the work ran:** from what every arm already records: system CPU from `/proc/schedstat` per cluster (M2 §3.2), minus the idle baseline, and each thread's CPU time over the window (`threads.csv`). M6 adds no sampling to this arm, so that Cpf differs from Cp in the allowed CPUs only.
  - **Process CPU for Cpf** counts cores of two kinds, as P's did (M5 §2).
- **Ct:**
  - **A new option,** `--time-rknn on`: each NPU worker reads `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` before and after each `rknn_run`. The run's summary gives the sum of the differences and the number of calls, over the frames whose `t4` is in the window (PROTOCOL §7.3).
  - **Why a separate arm:** the reads are two system calls per inference on the NPU worker, some 10 ms of CPU per window by a rough count. That is far below what three runs can resolve (a standard error of about 0.2 s for a difference of two means), so `Ct − C` is reported with its interval and is not expected to show the reads. C itself stays as it was measured before.
- **Unchanged arms stay unchanged.** With the new options at their defaults, B, Bd, C and Cp do what the code of `freeze-m3` does with the same options. §8 step 2 says which files may change, and step 3 checks it.

## 2. Hypotheses

All three use PROTOCOL §7.1's process CPU over the 120 s window and the means of the runs that count (§4). Each has a spec-literal and a practical verdict, as H1 had.

Each threshold comes with its reason, and with the decision rule's approximate error rates where earlier runs give a spread. The error rates assume independent runs and normal errors, and ignore that the spread differs between sessions.

### M6-H1: the conversion carries the saving

> At the main condition with 8 channels, moving the conversion to RGA3 while the CPU still copies into the NPU's input (Rc) gives at least 90% of C's saving against B.

- **Spec-literal verdict:** with `B`, `Rc` and `C` the means, *adopted* if `(B − Rc) / (B − C) ≥ 0.90`, otherwise *rejected*.
- **The threshold's reason:** M1.1's Bd saved about 5% of C's saving. Only part of that is in the threads where the copy runs: 0.71 s, about 2.9% of `B − C`. If Rc's copy costs what B's does, Rc keeps 95% to 97%. The threshold allows a loss of 10%, two to three and a half times that. A result below it means that Rc's bundle (the copy, its sync pair and a cached destination for RGA3) costs clearly more than B's copy did.
- **Error rates, approximate:** with M1.1's sd of 0.27 s (B), 0.26 s (C) and 0.67 s (Bd, taken for Rc), the standard error of the ratio is about 1.7 points. The rule then adopts with a probability of about 99.9% if the true share is 95%, and of about 0.1% if it is 85%. At 90% either verdict is about as likely as the other.
- **Mixed:** if the same ratio from `/proc/schedstat` (below) is on the other side of 0.90.

### M6-H2: removing the pinning raises `bench_main`'s process CPU

> At the main condition with 8 channels, C with the package's post-processing and its threads allowed every CPU (Cpf) uses at least 20% more process CPU than the same pinned to cpu4–7 (Cp).

- **Spec-literal verdict:** *adopted* if `(Cpf − Cp) / Cp ≥ 0.20`, otherwise *rejected*.
- **The threshold's reason:** in M5 the service as installed used 48.5% more than the service on cpu4–7. M5 already showed that restricting the package removes its excess over C. What M6 asks is whether `bench_main` shows a rise of that kind from the placement alone. 20% is less than half of M5's difference: a result under it would say that removing the pinning does not reproduce most of P's excess.
- **Error rates:** none are given. Where the scheduler puts Cpf's threads can differ between runs, and no run of `bench_main` without pinning exists to give a spread. With M5's sd for P and P4 the standard error of the ratio would be about 1.8 points; whether Cpf varies as little is not known.
- **Mixed:** if `Cpf − Cp` has the opposite sign on `/proc/schedstat`.
- **What an adoption says:** that the placement alone adds at least 20% to `bench_main`'s process CPU on this board. How much of P's 48.5% it reproduces is reported as `(Cpf − Cp) / Cp` next to 0.485, across sessions, without a verdict. Cpf is not the package (§7).

### M6-H3: a faster CPU path narrows the gap

> At the main condition with 8 channels, B with libyuv (By) uses at least 10% less process CPU than B.

- **Spec-literal verdict:** *adopted* if `(B − By) / B ≥ 0.10`, otherwise *rejected*.
- **The threshold's reason:** it is the size at which the comparison this project reports would change noticeably. With B at 43.7 s and C at 19.3 s, a By at 10% below B puts C's saving against the faster CPU path at 51% in place of 56%. What is known about the room: B's preprocessing threads use about 24.6 s, 56% of B, so that is the most By can save; 10% of B is 18% of those threads' time. How much libyuv removes is not known.
- **Error rates, approximate:** with M1.1's sd of B (0.27 s) for both arms, the standard error of `(B − By) / B` is about 0.5 points; the rule then adopts with a probability of about 2% if the true saving is 9%, and of about 98% if it is 11%. With the other sessions' sd of B (0.34 to 0.79 s) those are about 5% to 24%, and 95% to 77%.
- **Mixed:** if `B − By` has the opposite sign on `/proc/schedstat`.
- **If By is not run** (§1): both verdicts are *indeterminate*, and the report gives the equivalence results that stopped it.

### For all three

- **Practical verdict:** the spec-literal one, except that it is *indeterminate* in either of these cases:
  - in any run that counts of an arm the hypothesis names, some channel's inference rate is below 4.75 fps (PROTOCOL §7.4);
  - the mean drop rates (PROTOCOL §7.5) of two arms the hypothesis names differ by more than 1 percentage point. M2 §2 had this rule in one direction; here it holds in either: an arm must not pass by doing less work.
- **Secondary metric:** system CPU from `/proc/schedstat` with the idle baseline subtracted (M2 §3.2), used as each hypothesis's "Mixed" says. The label is in addition to the verdicts.
- **Missing runs:** if an arm a hypothesis names has fewer than 3 runs that count, its spec-literal verdict uses the runs that exist and is flagged, and its practical verdict is *indeterminate*. With no run that counts, both are *indeterminate*.

### What the hypotheses can and cannot tell

- **M6-H1 is close to what M1.1 already showed.** It tests the one cell M1.1 could not fill, and an adoption is the expected result. Its ratio compares bundles, not a pure "copy" (§1).
- **M6-H2 tests the scheduler on this board,** under this load, on this kernel, as M5-H1 did. Cpf's value can move with anything else that runs on the board.
- **M6-H3 is about one library called one way,** with B's filter and B's thread count. A rejection does not show that no CPU path is much faster than B's; an adoption does not show that By is the fastest. A path that filters less, such as taking single pixels, would be cheaper and is not what By is.
- **B stays the registered reference.** Whatever By shows, H1's verdict and M1's numbers stand. By is a further arm next to B, not its replacement.
- **One board, one image, one model, one point,** file-backed RTSP streams at 1080p.

## 3. Metrics

- **All arms are `bench_main`:** PROTOCOL §7's metrics, as M3 §1 and M2 §3.2 (`/proc/schedstat`) have them. No arm is measured from outside.
- **For Cpf:** PROTOCOL §7.1's split of process CPU by affinity is reported as it is computed and means nothing for this arm. Where its work ran is described by the per-cluster `/proc/schedstat` values, which are the whole system's, and by `threads.csv`.
- **For Ct,** in addition, over the window:
  - the sum of the NPU workers' thread CPU time across `rknn_run`, and its share of the run's process CPU;
  - the mean thread CPU time of one `rknn_run`, next to the mean wall time of the same stage, which every arm records (`t3 − t2c`);
  - the number of calls.
- **Latency** is PROTOCOL §7.3's for every arm. Arrival phases are not controlled at this point (M3 §2), so latency is described and not judged.

## 4. Runs, order and validity

- **Runs:** 8 arms × 3 repetitions = 24, in one sweep.
- **Order within a repetition:**
  1. B, Bd, Rc, C, By, Cp, Cpf, Ct
  2. Ct, Cpf, Cp, By, C, Rc, Bd, B
  3. By, B, Rc, C, Bd, Cpf, Cp, Ct

  - **How it was chosen,** before any run: the second is the first reversed, so that over these two every arm has the same mean position. The third keeps the arms of each hypothesis next to each other (By and B; B, Rc and C; Cpf and Cp). Three orders cannot balance eight arms; the report gives every run's position, and the paired differences are within repetitions.
  - **An arm that is not run** (§5) is left out, and the order of the others stands.
  - The runs of a repetition are never split across sessions. If a session ends inside a repetition, the whole repetition is rerun at the start of the next session, in the same order; the runs already finished are kept and flagged.
- **Sessions:** as M3 §3: 10 minutes idle with the soc-thermal and both system CPU baselines, the cooldown gate (PROTOCOL §9.2) before every run, and the environment recorded before every run (§9.1). The operator reports the ambient temperature at each session's start. A session of the whole sweep takes about 1 hour 35 minutes.
  - **A session ends** by a reboot, a cooling change or 30 minutes without a run. The operator ends one only between repetitions, or for a logged external failure (power, network link, stream server). Any other end inside a repetition is reported with its reason.
- **Inventory:** before a session starts, M3 §4's pinned keys must equal `docs/notes/inventory_m1.txt`. So By's backend and libyuv are built as a target of their own, with their include paths and definitions private to it; the pinned keys of the build do not change.
- **Validity:** PROTOCOL §9.3, rules 1 to 4, as in M3 §3, for every arm. One more rule for Cpf, as M5 §4's rule 5: the run is invalid if, in `env.json`'s `threads_at_window_start` or in `threads.csv` (the window's end), some pipeline thread is not allowed every CPU of 0 to 7, or the metrics thread is allowed any CPU but 0. For the other arms the affinities are what they were, and `threads.csv` shows them.
- **Handling:** an invalid run is repeated once, right after it, after the cooldown gate. If the repeat is invalid too, the run is invalid ×2 and counts as missing. Attempts count across sessions as `docs/notes/comparison.md` says. The CV flag of §9.3 applies to the process CPU of every arm a hypothesis names: above 10%, and for B and By also above 5%, half of M6-H3's threshold.
- **The runs that count:** per repetition and arm, the valid run of the session that completed the repetition.

## 5. What could go wrong, and what then

- **Rc's tensor is not C's byte for byte:** Rc has a defect. It is fixed and checked again before `freeze-m6`. If it cannot be made equal, Rc is not run, M6-H1 is *indeterminate*, and the report says why.
- **libyuv does not build, or By's functional check fails:** a defect of the build or of By's code; it is fixed before any comparison with B. A wrong channel order or band position is such a defect. A fix may change By's file or how libyuv is added to the build (§4), not libyuv's source, its CMake options or definitions, or its commit other than by §1's fallback.
- **By does not pass A5's criteria:** that is final. By is not run (§1, §2).
- **By passes but is slower than B:** it is run as registered. M6-H3 is then rejected, and that is a result.
- **Cpf's check fails,** or `--cores all` cannot be built as §1 says: Cpf is not run and M6-H2 is *indeterminate*. The same holds for Ct and its option, with no hypothesis affected.
- **Cpf drops frames or runs below 4.75 fps:** its runs stay valid if §4's rules hold. The practical verdict of M6-H2 is then *indeterminate*, and the drop rate is reported. Nothing is tuned.
- **Cpf's placement differs from run to run:** that is Cpf. The spread is reported; the CV flag may be set.
- **Every run of an arm is invalid by the same rule:** the arm has no run that counts, and a hypothesis that names it is *indeterminate*. The rule is not relaxed.
- **An unchanged arm is changed:** if a check of §8 step 3 finds that B, Bd, C or Cp no longer does what `freeze-m3`'s code does, the new code has a defect, and `freeze-m6` waits for its fix.
- **A defect after `freeze-m6`:** a value or a rule computed otherwise than this text says. It is fixed by an amendment, and the sweep is rerun whole. The report says when each defect was found and whether results had been looked at by then. Superseded runs stay under their records and are reported. A run that merely failed is not a defect but an invalid run.
- **Not changed, whatever any run shows, the checks of §8 step 3 included:**
  - §2's thresholds and verdict rules;
  - the point, the arms and what each arm is (§1), apart from the two fallbacks §1 names (Rc's heap, libyuv's commit);
  - §4's number of runs, its order, session, validity and handling rules, and the runs that count;
  - §3's metrics and §6's reported values;
  - the code once it is frozen.

## 6. Reporting

- **The hypotheses:** each with its two verdicts, its value, its "mixed" label or none, and §2's notes.
- **The four cells (question 1),** from the means of the runs that count, each as seconds and as a share of `B − C`:
  - the conversion's move to RGA3 with the CPU copy (`B − Rc`) and without it (`Bd − C`);
  - the cost of B's copy bundle (`B − Bd`) and of Rc's (`Rc − C`);
  - their difference, `(B − Bd) − (Rc − C)`, said to mix any interaction with the two bundles' different cache maintenance (§1);
  - the same by thread group, as [cpu_breakdown.md](../docs/notes/cpu_breakdown.md) has it.
- **The CPU path (question 2):** `(B − By) / B`, By's preprocessing threads next to B's, and C's saving against By, `(By − C) / By`, next to its saving against B.
- **The package's excess (question 3):** `(Cp − C) / C`, `(Cpf − Cp) / Cp`, where Cpf's work ran (§3), and, across sessions and labelled so, Cp and Cpf next to M5's P4 and P. P's and P4's `/proc/schedstat` values hold M5's measuring tool, about 1.45 s per window on cpu0; Cp's and Cpf's hold only the metrics thread, about 0.1 s.
- **`rknn_run` (the description):** §3's values for Ct, and `Ct − C`.
- **Paired differences,** over the repetitions that have both runs, with a descriptive 95% t interval (degrees of freedom: pairs − 1; none with fewer than 2 pairs), for process CPU, `/proc/schedstat` system CPU, and latency p50 and p99, of: `B − Rc`, `Bd − C`, `B − Bd`, `Rc − C`, `B − By`, `By − C`, `Cp − C`, `Cpf − Cp`, `Ct − C`, and `B − C`.
- **Descriptively, for every arm:** process CPU, both system CPU measures with the per-cluster split, latency p50 and p99, the stage times, arrival phases, inference rate, drop rate, the highest temperature, VmRSS, and each run's position in the session.
- **B and C next to M1's, M3's and M5's values** at this point, and the values of every run, valid or not.
- **Said with them:** that latency in an unstaggered run depends on arrival phases that are not controlled, and that Cpf's and P's process CPU count cores of two kinds.
- **Release:** a report `docs/report/M6.md` with its tables, under its own tag.

## 7. What M6 does not tell

- **The rest of the package's difference from C:** the build, the JSON lines and the socket, the service, and being measured from outside (M5 §1) are not separated. Cpf and M5's P ran in different sessions, and their comparison is descriptive.
- **A pure cost of "the copy":** each cell is a bundle (§1). What RGA3's driver does for a cached destination is inside Rc's value and is not separated.
- **Anything at another point.** The auxiliary condition (5 channels at 30 fps) is not run, to keep the sweep in one session of a length the earlier milestones had. #62 proposed it; M1.1 found the copy's share smaller still there. It is also the point where M2 saw the NPU workers save more per inference than the post-processing stage's own drop, so Ct does not address that observation.
- **The best possible CPU path:** By is one library, called one way, with B's filter and thread count. #63's optional context, the CPU cost of other Rockchip pipelines (ffmpeg-rockchip's `scale_rkrga`, GStreamer's elements), is not measured.
- **What `rknn_run` does with its CPU time:** Ct gives how much, not what for. Output bindings that differ in layout or type are not compared; #60 stays open for that part.
- **Kernel-side cost of RGA3, power, memory traffic:** as before (#58, #59).

## 9. Amendments

- **Numbering:** M6's amendments go to AMENDMENTS.md under the next free numbers. The latest is A16.
- **PROTOCOL §15 applies** with `freeze-m6` in place of `freeze-m1`:
  - Before `freeze-m6`, measurements affected by a change are redone from scratch.
  - After it, a change to `src/`, `tools/`, `tests/`, the root `CMakeLists.txt` or libyuv's commit that an M6 run used invalidates every M6 run, and the sweep is rerun whole, for all arms.
  - Superseded runs stay under their records and are reported.
- **Releases:** every amendment made after `prereg-m6` is published as its own Release `amend-A<n>`.
