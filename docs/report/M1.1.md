# M1.1 report: 4K input and the optional designs (exploratory)

**What M1.1 is:** after M1 ([M1.md](M1.md)), the same board and pipelines ran four more designs:

- the optional designs of PROTOCOL §13, registered at `prereg-v1` before any measurement;
- a 4K design, registered in A11.

Nothing here is judged. No hypothesis is tested, and none of M1's verdicts changes.

| Design | Its question | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| **B-direct** (§13, A12) | How much of C's CPU saving comes from removing the last copy, and how much from removing the CPU conversion? | Bd, which drops B's last copy but still converts on the CPU, saved 2.8% of B's process CPU at 8 channels and 0.8% at 5 channels × 30 fps. C saved 55.7% and 71.4%. So dropping the copy gives about 5% and 1% of C's saving. The rest is what separates C from Bd: the conversion on RGA3, with anything else C does differently. |
| **B with K = 4** (§13, A12) | Is the shared structure, two preprocessing threads, unfair to B? | No sign of it at 8 channels. With four threads, B used 2.6% more process CPU and its p99 was 49.6 against 45.1 ms. Frames waited less for a preprocessing thread and longer for the NPU. |
| **A55 only** (§13, A12) | B against C on the four A55 cores | Both held 5 fps on all 8 channels. C used 65.7% less process CPU (B 150.6 s, C 51.7 s per 120 s), and its p99 was 54.4 against 98.1 ms. One repetition. |
| **4K input** (A11) | B against C on 3840×2160 streams | C used 59 to 60% less process CPU, and its p99 was lower at 1, 2, 4 and 8 channels. Equivalence is not claimed at 4K. |
| **Arrival phases** (found after the fact) | Why does latency differ between runs of one arm? | How close together the channels' frames arrive is fixed within a run. It changes between runs and is not controlled by the protocol. Runs whose frames came together tended to wait longer for the NPU. This bears on the latency comparisons of M1 and M1.1; no consistent relation to CPU was seen. |

## How it was measured

- **Registration:**
  - §13 registered its designs at `prereg-v1`. A12 then fixed how to build and run them, after M1's results and before any of their runs (Release `amend-A12`, 2026-11-04T05:30:51Z).
  - The 4K design is A11, part of `freeze-m1` (2026-11-03T06:58:17Z).
  - `tools/check_prereg_order.py` checks that every run started after its registration.
  - The analysis of A12's designs ([analysis.md](../notes/analysis.md#the-designs-of-13-a12)) was committed while their first session ran, before any of their metrics had been looked at.
  - Some readings came after the data had been seen. Two are labelled where they appear: a55's CPU by thread affinity, and the arrival phases. The readings of the stage times ([M1_stages.md](M1_stages.md)), such as where the time went in Bk4 or on the A55 cores, are exploratory and after the fact too, as they were in M1.
- **As in M1:** conditions, fixed clocks, cooling, 20 s warm-up and 120 s window, the cooldown gate, and the validity rules.
  - A12's designs use M1's 1080p videos.
  - The 4K design uses ①, ② and ④, re-encoded at 3840×2160.
- **The arms:**
  - **Bd (B-direct):** B's OpenCV code writes straight into cached dma32 buffers that are registered with the NPU and synced with `DMA_BUF_IOCTL_SYNC`. So B's last `memcpy`, into the bound input, is gone.
    - Before the sessions, `tools/bdirect_check` found Bd's input tensor equal to B's byte for byte on 100 frames, with equal outputs ([check.csv](../../results/check/20261104T053334Z_bdirect_check/check.csv)).
  - **Bk4:** B with 4 preprocessing threads instead of 2, and 13 input slots instead of 11.
  - **a55:** B and C with every pipeline thread on cpu0–3. The metrics thread runs on cpu4.
- **Controls rerun:** A12 changed the shared core: K and the cores became options. So each design reran its own B and C, in its own session and with the same binary, and no A12 value is set against M1's.
  - A default run of B with that binary had M1's threads, affinities and queues ([default_b](../../results/check/20261104T053324Z_default_b)).
- **Sessions,** all on 2026-11-04 (UTC):

  | Session | Design | Time (UTC) | Runs | Code | Ambient |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | [4k](../../results/m1.1/20261104T005719Z_4k) | A11 | 01:07–02:30 | 24 | `b00c4cc` | 25 °C |
  | [k4](../../results/m1.1/20261104T053416Z_k4) | A12 | 05:44–06:03 | 6 | `cdb309e` | 27 °C |
  | [a55](../../results/m1.1/20261104T060352Z_a55) | A12 | 06:14–06:19 | 2 | `cdb309e` | 27 °C |
  | [bdirect_main](../../results/m1.1/20261104T061958Z_bdirect_main) | A12 | 06:30–06:59 | 9 | `cdb309e` | 27 °C |
  | [bdirect_aux](../../results/m1.1/20261104T065947Z_bdirect_aux) | A12 | 07:09–07:41 | 9 | `cdb309e` | 27 °C |

  - **Every run was valid.** There are no invalid ×2 records, no repeats and no lost attempts. No sample reached a trip point.
  - **Times:** from the first run's start to the last run's end.
  - **Ambient:** reported by the operator, not measured: 25 °C before the 4K session, and 27 °C once, at 05:31 UTC, for all four of A12's sessions.
- **Numbers:** every metric of every point is in [M1_tables.md](M1_tables.md), the stage times in [M1_stages.md](M1_stages.md), and the arrival phases in [M1_phases.md](M1_phases.md). Values below are the mean ± sample sd over 3 runs, per 120 s window, unless marked.

## B-direct: what the last copy costs

| 8 channels, main | B | C | Bd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process CPU (s) | 44.04 ± 0.27 | 19.49 ± 0.26 | 42.79 ± 0.67 |
| p50 latency (ms) | 35.6 ± 1.4 | 29.1 ± 0.5 | 21.3 ± 0.4 |
| p99 latency (ms) | 48.1 ± 1.0 | 41.0 ± 4.1 | 29.0 ± 2.6 |

| 5 channels, 30 fps | B | C | Bd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process CPU (s) | 130.95 ± 0.56 | 37.45 ± 1.41 | 129.85 ± 0.66 |
| p50 latency (ms) | 22.6 ± 2.4 | 17.7 ± 0.2 | 22.2 ± 1.2 |
| p99 latency (ms) | 30.5 ± 3.2 | 22.8 ± 3.0 | 32.7 ± 1.8 |

- **CPU:** against B, Bd saved 2.8% (8 channels) and 0.8% (5 channels at 30 fps). C saved 55.7% and 71.4%.
  - So dropping the last copy gives about 5% and 1% of C's saving. The rest, about 95% and 99%, is what separates C from Bd: the resize, colour conversion and letterbox move to RGA3, along with anything else C does differently, such as the CPU no longer reading the decoder's buffer. This design cannot split that part further.
  - The stage times fit this. Bd's bind takes 0.0 ms against B's 0.1 ms, and its preparation 5.1 ms, like B's 5.2 ms, while C's takes 1.7 ms.
  - Bd's largest RSS was 7% below B's (224.6 against 242.3 MB), probably because it has no separate CPU input buffers.
- **Latency at 8 channels:** Bd's p99 was the lowest of the three. Its stage times do not point to the path:
  - preparation and `rknn_run` took as long as B's, and binding 0.1 ms less;
  - its frames waited less. Their median Q2 wait (for an NPU worker) was 0.0 ms, against B's 8.6 and C's 11.5 ms.
  - In Bd's runs, the channels' frames arrived spread over 31 to 48 ms of each 200 ms, against 6 to 18 ms in B's and C's runs ([arrival phases](#arrival-phases-found-after-the-fact)). That is a candidate reason, not a tested one.
- **Latency at 5 channels × 30 fps,** where the three arms' spreads overlapped, Bd's p99 was close to B's: 32.7 against 30.5 ms.

## B with K = 4

| 8 channels, main | B | Bk4 |
|---|---|---|
| Process CPU (s) | 43.85 ± 0.27 | 45.00 ± 0.22 |
| p50 latency (ms) | 32.1 ± 3.4 | 34.0 ± 1.4 |
| p99 latency (ms) | 45.1 ± 2.4 | 49.6 ± 3.2 |
| MPP hold p50 (ms) | 8.5 ± 1.5 | 5.6 ± 0.1 |

- **What changed with four threads:**
  - A decoded frame barely waited for a preprocessing thread: Q1 wait p50 0.1 against 3.1 ms. So the decoder's buffers were released sooner (MPP hold).
  - It then waited longer for an NPU worker: Q2 wait p50 10.9 against 7.5 ms, p99 27.8 against 16.4 ms. So the total did not drop.
  - Process CPU rose 2.6%.
- **So four threads did not lower B's latency at 8 channels, in these 3 runs each.** The time moved from the wait for a preprocessing thread to the wait for an NPU worker. This fits the queue before the NPU setting the latency, as M1 suggested at its 5-channel × 30 fps point ([M1.md](M1.md#h2-tail-latency-at-30-fps)). It was not tested.
- **Arrival spreads were alike for both arms:** 6 to 15 ms.

## A55 cores only

| 8 channels, main, cpu0–3, one run each | B | C |
|---|---|---|
| Process CPU (s) | 150.6 | 51.7 |
| p50 latency (ms) | 67.9 | 38.0 |
| p99 latency (ms) | 98.1 | 54.4 |
| MPP hold p50 (ms) | 48.5 | 2.3 |

- **Both held 5 fps** on every channel, with no drops.
- **C used 65.7% less process CPU.** B used 1.26 of the four A55 cores (31%), C 0.43 (11%).
- **Where B's time went:** its preparation took 21.2 ms on an A55 core (C's: 1.7 ms). Its frames waited for one of the two preprocessing threads: Q1 wait p50 26.3 ms, p99 55.9 ms. C's frames waited for an NPU worker instead: Q2 wait p50 16.1 ms.
- **CPU by core** (added after the table was seen): `bench_main` counts A76 and A55 CPU only for M1's affinities, so here everything is "unattributed".
  - By thread affinity, from `threads.csv`, B's threads on cpu0–3 used 150.62 s and the metrics thread on cpu4 0.03 s.
  - For C, the same split was 51.63 s and 0.04 s.
- **One repetition:** the variation between runs is not known.

## 4K input (A11)

| 8 channels, main | B | C |
|---|---|---|
| Process CPU (s) | 96.2 | 39.5 |
| Preparation p50 (ms) | 12.6 | 6.1 |
| p99 latency (ms) | 32.4 | 25.3 |

- **CPU:** C used 59 to 60% less process CPU than B at every count (1, 2, 4, 8). At 8 channels that is about twice the 1080p values in M1.
- **Latency:** C's p99 was lower at every count.
- **Every run** held 5 fps per channel, with no drops.
- **Almost no queue at 8 channels:** no frame waited more than 0.9 ms for an NPU worker. The channels' frames were spread over 100 to 152 ms of each 200 ms, against 7 to 23 ms in M1's 8-channel runs at 1080p.
  - That is a candidate reason why the 8-channel p99 at 4K was below M1's at 1080p (B 32.4 against 47.4 ms, C 25.3 against 44.0 ms), although 4K's preparation takes longer. It was not tested, and 4K differs from M1 in other ways too.
  - At 8 channels, the stream server discarded frames in the warm-up of every run ("reader is too slow"). This may have scattered the phases. The records do not show whether it did.
- **Server warnings:** the server's lines came 2 to 5 s after each 8-channel run's start, in the warm-up, and never in a window ([runs.tsv](../../results/m1.1/20261104T005719Z_4k/runs.tsv)).
- **Tensors:** on 1,000 frames of ① at 4K, C's tensors are further from B's than R's are: MAE 4.52 against 1.88, minimum PSNR 28.6 against 37.3 dB. Total boxes differ from B's by +1.46% for C and +0.04% for R ([recheck.txt](../../results/m1.1/20261104T023058Z_4k_tensors/recheck.txt)). Under A11, equivalence under A5 is not claimed at 4K.

## Arrival phases (found after the fact)

This section was not planned. It was found while writing this report, after every run, and is exploratory.

- **What a phase is:** a channel infers 1 frame in 6 in the main condition and every frame in the auxiliary. So its inferred frames come every 200 ms or every 33.3 ms. Their position in that period is the channel's phase.
- **Phases are fixed within a run.** In all 107 runs of [M1_phases.md](M1_phases.md), no channel's phase moved by more than 2.4 ms from the first sixth of its frames in the window to any later sixth. Those runs are the runs with more than one channel in M1's main, auxiliary and reference sweeps and in M1.1.
- **They are not controlled.** The phases are set when the channels start. The protocol neither controls nor records them.
- **Spread:** for each run, [M1_phases.md](M1_phases.md) gives the spread: the shortest part of the period that holds every channel's phase.
  - A small spread means the frames come in bursts.
  - Bursts can queue for the two preprocessing threads and the three NPU workers.
- **Seen:**
  - **M1, 8 channels:** B's run with spread 23.0 ms had p99 41.0 ms. Its other two, with 6.7 and 7.2 ms, had 50.5 and 50.7 ms.
  - **M1, 32 channels:**
    - In every run, B's channels were spread over 103 to 125 ms and C's over 59 to 67 ms.
    - C's frames waited longer for an NPU worker: Q2 wait p50 38 to 40 against 20 to 25 ms. Its p99 was higher: 140 to 146 against 115 to 129 ms.
    - M1's report claims no cause for C's slower saturation. The phases are a candidate, not a tested one.
  - **M1, 4 channels:** C's run with spread 6.3 ms had p99 26.3 ms; its others, with 11.7 and 12.7 ms, had 21.1 and 20.6 ms.
  - **B-direct and 4K:** above.
  - **H2's point, 5 channels × 30 fps:** in M1, C's run with the highest p99, 30.5 ms, had spread 4.1 ms. C's other two runs had 13.3 and 14.7 ms, with p99 23.4 and 23.7 ms. B's runs follow less clearly: its run with spread 16.6 ms had p99 28.3 ms.
- **What it means:**
  - At multi-channel points, latency went with a factor that the protocol neither controlled nor recorded. That factor differed between arms: at 32 channels in M1, and for Bd here. So some latency differences between arms may come from it rather than from the paths.
  - These are associations, over 107 runs with three runs per arm and point, and not every point follows them clearly.
  - Why the phases differ between arms is not known. This report claims no cause.
  - Process CPU varied little between runs of an arm: its CV was at most 6.9% in M1.1, and below 5% in M1. No consistent relation between it and the phases was seen, and none was tested.
  - M1's verdicts stand as registered.
  - To separate this factor, a protocol would have to fix the phases or randomise and record them. That is a candidate for a later milestone.

## Data quality

- **Flagged, CV above 10% (§9.3):** p99 latency at bdirect_main C (10.1%), bdirect_aux B (10.6%) and bdirect_aux C (13.0%). Nothing at 4K was flagged. Process CPU's CV stayed at or below 3.8% in A12's designs and 6.9% at 4K.
- **Warnings:** none, except the 4K server warnings above.
- **a55's split:** added under a55's table after the table was seen ([analysis.md](../notes/analysis.md#the-designs-of-13-a12)). It changes no value.
- **System CPU is not used here.**
  - The per-core counts from `/proc/stat` varied much more than process CPU between runs of one arm. For C at 8 channels in bdirect_main, they ranged from 30.0 to 67.2 s in total, while process CPU stayed within 19.2 to 19.7 s.
  - Some runs counted less than process CPU. In a55, B's system total was 146.3 s against 150.6 s of process CPU.
  - M1's runs show the same. C at 8 channels: system 9.4 to 13.0 s, process 18.2 to 18.8 s.
  - So this report reads no result from system CPU.

## Threats to validity

- **Fixed after M1's results:** how A12's designs were built and run was fixed after M1's results; the designs themselves were registered before. A12 discloses the short development runs that came before it.
- **A different binary:** A12's designs ran with a binary that differs from M1's. They are compared only with the controls of their own session.
- **Arrival phases:** they may confound the latency comparisons between arms at multi-channel points (above). The analysis is after the fact.
- **a55:** one repetition, and its CPU by core comes from `threads.csv`.
- **4K:**
  - equivalence is not claimed;
  - the stream server discarded frames in the warm-up of 8-channel runs;
  - the ambient temperature was 25 °C, against 27 °C for A12.
- **As in M1:** one board, one model and one runtime. The ambient temperature was reported by the operator, not measured.

## Reproducing this report

From a clone of the repository, with Python 3 and numpy:

```sh
python3 tools/m1_report.py --out docs/report/M1_tables.md --json docs/report/M1_summary.json
python3 tools/m1_stages.py --out docs/report/M1_stages.md
python3 tools/m1_phases.py --out docs/report/M1_phases.md
python3 tools/check_prereg_order.py                    # 0 problems
git diff --exit-code docs/report                       # the same files
```
