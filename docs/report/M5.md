# M5 report: the packaged path at H1's point

**What M5 is:** a measurement of the zero-copy path as a user installs it. The package `rkzc` 0.1.1 ran as its systemd service next to the benchmark's B and C, in one session, at H1's point: 8 channels, 5 inferences per second each, a 120 s window. The arms, the two hypotheses, the metrics and the order were registered before any run ([prereg/M5.md](../../prereg/M5.md), [A16](../../prereg/AMENDMENTS.md)).

| Arm | What ran |
|---|---|
| B | `bench_main --variant B`: preprocessing on the CPU |
| C | `bench_main --variant C`: the zero-copy path |
| P | the service `rkzc-detect`, as the package installs it |
| P4 | the same service with one line of systemd configuration, `CPUAffinity=4-7` (the A76 cores) |

**Outcome: both hypotheses are adopted.**

| Hypothesis | B (s) | Arm (s) | (B − arm) / B | Threshold | Spec-literal | Practical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M5-H1:** P's process CPU is at least 25% lower than B's | 43.733 | 26.907 | 38.5% | 25% | **adopted** | **adopted** |
| **M5-H2:** P4's process CPU is at least 25% lower than B's | 43.733 | 18.113 | 58.6% | 25% | **adopted** | **adopted** |

Each value is the mean of 3 runs, per 120 s window. Neither result is *mixed*: the secondary metric, system CPU from `/proc/schedstat`, has the same sign (B 45.671 s, P 30.134 s, P4 21.542 s). No flag is set: every arm has 3 runs that count, no channel ran below 4.75 fps, and the CV of process CPU is 0.8% for B, 1.4% for P and 1.6% for P4.

**The headline values, which have no threshold (M5 §6):**

| Arm | (arm − C) / C | (B − arm) / (B − C) |
|---|---|---|
| P | +39.8% | 68.7% |
| P4 | −5.9% | 104.6% |

- **As installed, the service used about 40% more process CPU than C,** and kept about two thirds of C's saving against B. C itself was 56.0% below B in this session (19.250 s).
- **Restricted to the A76 cores, it used about as much as C.** The paired difference P4 − C is −1.14 s with a descriptive 95% interval of ±1.62 s, which includes zero.
- **These differences mix several causes** that M5 does not separate (M5 §1, and "What the differences mix" below). In particular, P's CPU seconds were spent on two kinds of cores, so P's value is not the same quantity as the other three.

### Where P's work ran

- **On cpu0–3:** 42%, 51% and 44% of P's CPU time in the three runs (mean 46%), by each thread's last CPU at the one-second samples. In P4 it was 0%.
- **By thread,** from the same samples, mean of 3 runs. The share is that of the group's total, given as its range over the 3 runs; single threads range far wider. 30 of the 32 threads are listed: the other two, named `rkzc-detect`, used less than 0.01 s.

  | Threads | P (s) | Share of it on cpu0–3 | P4 (s) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `npu-0` to `npu-2`, the three inference workers | 10.65 | 51% to 58% | 6.53 |
  | `dec-1` to `dec-8`, one per channel | 10.54 | 28% to 56% | 7.41 |
  | `mpp_dec_parser`, 8 threads of MPP | 4.09 | 25% to 48% | 3.13 |
  | `mpp_dec_hal`, 8 threads of MPP | 1.11 | 49% to 54% | 0.63 |
  | `pre`, 2 threads | 0.42 | 43% to 55% | 0.32 |
  | `monitor` | 0.09 | | 0.06 |

- **What this shows and does not:** P spent more CPU seconds than P4 in each of these six groups, and close to half of P's seconds were on the slower cores. An A55 second does less work than an A76 second, so more seconds on them is expected. M5 has no arm that would show how much of P − P4 the placement explains. Other things differ too: P's threads move between clusters, and they share cpu0 with the tool.
- **Against what the plan knew:** the plan said that, by M1.1's value for a pipeline wholly on cpu0–3 (51.7 s against 18.56 s), the threshold would be crossed at roughly 40% of the *work* on the A55 cores. P's 46% is a share of CPU *seconds*, which is another quantity: by the same ratio it corresponds to roughly a quarter of the work, where that arithmetic gives about 26 to 27 s. P measured 26.9 s, against the threshold's 32.8 s. This is arithmetic on one earlier run of another arrangement, not a test of why P costs what it does.

### What process CPU leaves out

- **`bench_main`'s metrics thread** is inside B's and C's process CPU. Without it, B is 43.637 s and C 19.140 s (0.10 s and 0.11 s less).
- **The tool** that reads the socket and samples the service is outside P's and P4's process CPU. Its own CPU over the window was 1.45 s with P and 1.49 s with P4. This is the reader and the sampler together; the reader alone was not measured.
- **Both adjustments at once,** as a bound and not as a registered value: adding the whole tool to the service and taking the metrics thread out of B and C gives P 28.36 s and P4 19.61 s, that is 35.0% and 55.1% below B, and 48.2% and 2.4% above C. Both hypotheses would hold by this count too.

## What the differences mix

P and P4 differ from C in several ways at once (M5 §1), and M5 separates none of them:

- **Post-processing:** the package uses M2's faster one; C here uses M1's.
- **CPU placement:** `bench_main` pins its pipeline to cpu4–7. P pins nothing; P4 restricts every thread to cpu4–7.
- **Start:** the service's channels each start when their source opens, so their arrival phases are of another kind than `bench_main`'s.
- **What the program does besides:** no timeline and no per-frame records, but box mapping, one JSON line per frame, the socket, a monitor thread and the per-frame read of the counters.
- **Build and libraries:** `-fPIC`, hidden visibility, two replaced headers, and stripped copies of FFmpeg's libraries.
- **How it is run:** as a systemd service under its own user.
- **Who measures:** `bench_main` measures itself; the service is measured from outside.

So "P4 used about as much as C" does not mean the library costs nothing: a faster post-processing and a cost elsewhere can cancel, and M5 cannot tell.

## Threats to validity

- **One board, one image, one model, one point, one day.** File-backed RTSP streams at 1080p. Nothing here covers another board, camera, resolution or frame rate ([#70](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/70)).
- **One session, three runs per arm.** The intervals are descriptive, from 3 pairs.
- **M5-H2 was a lenient test,** as the plan said in advance: with C at 57% below B, an arm passes at 25%. Its adoption does not say that the package kept all of the saving; the headline value says how much.
- **M5-H1 tests the package together with the scheduler.** Where the kernel placed P's threads is this board's kernel and this load. Another kernel, or other work on the board, can place them otherwise, and P's value would move with it.
- **The threshold of 25% has no recorded rationale** ([#71](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/71)). It is H1's, taken over unchanged. Both arms cleared it by more than 13 points, so no nearby threshold would change a verdict.
- **The tool shares cpu0 with P,** whose threads also ran there. In B, C and P4 nothing of the pipeline runs on cpu0.
- **Latency is not the same measurement for the service.** Its end point `out_ns` is later than `bench_main`'s `t4` by the box mapping and the wait for the callback lock, and its warm-up starts later (M5 §3). Arrival phases are not controlled ([#64](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/64)).
- **Kernel-side CPU** is covered only by system CPU. For both hypotheses `/proc/schedstat` agrees with process CPU in sign and size. For P4 − C the two differ in sign (−1.14 s and +0.24 s), both with intervals that include zero; system CPU holds the tool's 1.5 s for P4 and only the metrics thread's 0.1 s for C. `/proc/stat` cannot be read ([#59](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/59)).
- **The room temperature** is the operator's report, once for the session ([#68](https://github.com/eunkyo3/rk3588s-zero-copy/issues/68)).
- **Seen before `freeze-m5`:** the check runs of M5 §8 step 3, with 10 s windows, whose values A16 lists (P 2.25 s against B 3.69 s and C 1.63 s in the last one). The threshold, the arms, the metrics and the validity rules were fixed by `prereg-m5`, before the service's first start, and nothing that M5 §5 fixes changed because of the checks.
- **One defect before `freeze-m5`, and A16's readings.** The first check session (2026-11-20) showed a defect in the check "The tool against `bench_main`": the tool's reads did not cover the end of `bench_main`'s window. Commit `a8eaa31` fixed it, A16 changed one sentence of M5 §8 step 3 for it, and every check was redone. The 10 s values of the first check session had been seen by then. That code runs in check sessions only, not in the sweep, and the change of `a8eaa31` was not reviewed separately. A16 also fixed twelve readings of M5.md where its text leaves a choice, such as the CV flag's 10% and which samples are "in the window"; they were in the tool before its first run.
- **The package's version of 0.1.1 was not installed in a clean image.** 0.1.0 was; the packaging files are unchanged.

## What M5 does not tell (§7)

- **Why P differs from C:** see above.
- **The cost of other uses of the library:** a Python callback, several readers, stdout as the output.
- **Reconnection under load, long runs, memory growth:** the window is 120 s on streams that do not fail.
- **Anything new about B, or about H1's verdict:** B is here as H1's reference, and M1's results are not judged again.

## Reproducing this report

```sh
python lib/measure/m5_order.py
python lib/measure/m5_summary.py --sweep > docs/report/M5_summary.txt
```

`m5_summary.py` needs numpy; on Windows, set `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` for the redirect. The measurement itself is described in [lib/measure/README.md](../../lib/measure/README.md); it needs the board, the stream PC and the package of the Release `rkzc-v0.1.1`.
